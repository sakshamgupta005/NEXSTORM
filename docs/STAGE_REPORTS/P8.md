# Stage Report P8: Amritsar Data Foundation, Spatial Matching & Unified Risk Architecture

## 1. Executive Summary

Stage P8 establishes the empirical data feasibility audit, geodetic matching foundation, and unified risk architecture for **NEXSTORM**, preparing the repository to scale across its three equal pillars:
1. **Area-level risk assessment**: Retaining and expanding the verified 250m regular grid susceptibility and multi-window Rainfall Trigger Index (RTI).
2. **Building-level exposure assessment**: Associating discrete building footprints with macro-catchment hazard evidence while strictly refusing to make unsupported claims regarding indoor water depth or structural damage.
3. **Road-level risk assessment and flood-aware routing**: Associating road network segments with catchment susceptibility, railway underpass vulnerability, and fresh community observations to compare candidate routes by waterlogging exposure.

In strict adherence to the governing directive and ponytail guidelines, **P8 is an audit and architecture phase**. It avoids premature full-stack feature sprawl, preserves all working functionality, introduces lightweight, pure TypeScript spatial foundation utilities, and specifies rigorous data contracts without manufacturing synthetic data or fake production datasets.

## 2. File Change Ledger

### Created Modules & Architecture
- `src/lib/spatial/types.ts`: Core geodetic coordinates, bounding boxes, match quality enums, observation status, and unified risk product contracts (`PointGridMatch`, `BuildingExposureAssessment`, `RoadSegmentRiskAssessment`, `RouteRiskEvaluation`).
- `src/lib/spatial/crs.ts`: Pure TypeScript WGS84 (EPSG:4326) $\leftrightarrow$ UTM Zone 43N (EPSG:32643) geodetic forward and inverse Transverse Mercator projections and Euclidean metric distance calculations, verified to sub-millimeter precision against GDAL/pyproj.
- `src/lib/spatial/point-in-polygon.ts`: Deterministic ray-casting point-in-polygon engine supporting GeoJSON Polygon and MultiPolygon geometries with bounding-box pre-filtering and collinear edge tolerances.
- `src/lib/spatial/grid-matcher.ts`: Spatial grid matching engine mapping arbitrary WGS84 points to regular 250m grid cells, returning exact cell IDs, susceptibility scores, and centroid offsets, with honest out-of-bounds rejection.
- `src/lib/spatial/road-matcher.ts`: Orthogonal projection engine mapping point coordinates onto road polyline segments in UTM metric space, enforcing a strict 30m snapping tolerance and preserving an honest `unmatched` state when beyond tolerance.
- `src/lib/spatial/contracts.ts`: Canonical contract builder functions (`buildBuildingExposureAssessment`, `buildRoadSegmentRiskAssessment`, `evaluateRouteRisk`) enforcing scientific disclaimers and risk penalty scoring.
- `src/lib/spatial/index.ts`: Module export index.
- `tests/unit/spatial/spatial-foundation.test.ts`: Automated test suite (13 tests) verifying geodetic transformations, round-trip conversions, PIP ray-casting, real Amritsar grid snapping (SSSS Chowk, Hall Gate), 30m road snapping tolerance, and unified product contracts.

## 3. Spatial Dataset Inventory & Feasibility Findings

### 3.1 Area & Terrain Foundation
- **Amritsar Susceptibility Grid**: Verified operational at `public/data/cities/amritsar/susceptibility.geojson` (1,998 cells, 250m resolution, $62,500\text{ m}^2$ per cell). Secondary GNDU campus view verified at 50m resolution (432 cells).
- **Copernicus DEM GLO-30**: 30m posting in `pipeline/inputs/dem.tif`. Used for terrain slope flatness, relative height, and sink depressions via morphological filtering.
- **ESA WorldCover 2021**: 10m posting in `pipeline/inputs/worldcover.tif`. Built-up land cover classification (class 50) used for impervious surface fraction ($f_{\text{builtUp}}$).
- **Rainfall Climatology**: ERA5-Land 0.1° (~10km) hourly reanalysis across 15 monsoon seasons (2010–2024 JJAS) calibrated statutory thresholds ($T_{1\text{h}}=3.5\text{mm}, T_{3\text{h}}=8.9\text{mm}, T_{24\text{h}}=64.5\text{mm}, T_{72\text{h}}=44.8\text{mm}$).

### 3.2 Building Data: The OSM Sparsity Gap
- **Current Repository Status**: `pipeline/inputs/osm_features.gpkg` contains only **452 building polygons** (419 are generic `building: yes`). This represents $<0.15\%$ of actual urban structures in Amritsar.
- **Candidate External Dataset**: **Google Open Buildings V3** (CC-BY-4.0 / ODC-BY) provides $>350,000$ building footprints for Amritsar with polygon geometry, centroid, area ($m^2$), and detection confidence ($\in [0.65, 1.0]$).
- **Scientific Guardrails**: NEXSTORM strictly separates **Exposure** (spatial intersection with a high-susceptibility cell) from **Vulnerability** (indoor water depth, plinth height, basement presence, structural damage). These unobserved parameters cannot be inferred from 2D satellite footprints.

### 3.3 Road Network & Routing Feasibility
- **Current Repository Status**: `pipeline/inputs/osm_features.gpkg` contains **14,855 road segments** in WGS84 LineString format (12,426 residential, 1,128 service, 236 tertiary, 183 primary, 152 trunk, 123 secondary).
- **Topology Gap**: Road segments are currently flat geometric lines lacking a directed topological routing graph (nodes, edge connectivity, turn restrictions, one-way flags, or elevation profiles).
- **Routing Engine Evaluation**: Valhalla (MIT, dynamic costing models in C++) and GraphHopper (Apache 2.0, custom weighting models) are superior to standard OSRM Contraction Hierarchies, as they allow dynamic penalty cost evaluation during pathfinding without rebuilding graph hierarchies.
