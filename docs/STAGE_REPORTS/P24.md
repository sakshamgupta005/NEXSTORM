## 1. Executive Summary & Mission Scope

NEXSTORM was expanded in P23 from an Amritsar-only implementation into a multi-city platform with dynamic registry discovery, configuration schemas, and isolated alert repositories. The mission of **P24** is to rigorously audit the spatial artifacts, terrain features, and susceptibility distributions generated during the Jalandhar bootstrap, evaluate whether risk scores are comparable across cities, establish machine-readable quality observability, build an authentic evidence collection plan for Jalandhar, and enforce strict calibration gates.

### Core Scientific & Operational Verdicts:
1. **Zero Ground-Truth Fabrication:** No historical flood incidents or artificial validation metrics were manufactured for Jalandhar. Jalandhar remains strictly categorized as `evidence_insufficient` with external alert dispatch prohibited.
2. **Provenance Honesty Established:** An independent code audit revealed that P23's Jalandhar prototype derived terrain and urban morphology features from **analytic coordinate-decay equations** rather than ingested Copernicus DEM or OpenStreetMap raster/vector layers. This finding has been codified in machine-readable quality reports, removing any misleading impressions of physical sensor sampling.
3. **Cross-City Score Non-Equivalence Proven:** Susceptibility scores are ordinal within each city's boundary and **cannot be treated as comparable probabilities between cities**. In Jalandhar, 93.2% of cells are compressed into the mid-band ($[0.33, 0.66]$) due to flatness and low-point proxy skewness, whereas Amritsar exhibits an uncompressed distribution ($[0.0, 1.0]$, std $0.1697$).
4. **Machine-Readable Data Quality Observability:** Introduced `pipeline/scripts/17_city_quality_audit.py`, generating `quality_report.json` for every registered city. Distinguishes fatal technical errors from warnings and evidence limitations, integrating directly into the readiness state machine.

## 3. Cross-City Feature Comparability Audit (Phase 2)

We conducted a statistical comparison between Amritsar's physically sampled terrain model and Jalandhar's exploratory prototype:

```
AMRITSAR SUSCEPTIBILITY (Calibrated Baseline):
  Range: [0.0000, 1.0000], Mean: 0.4225, Std: 0.1697
  Bands: Low (32.23%) | Mid (59.96%) | High (7.81%)
  Features: Sampled from 30m DEM, 10m LandCover, 277k building footprints.

JALANDHAR SUSCEPTIBILITY (Exploratory Prototype):
  Range: [0.2346, 0.7508], Mean: 0.4557, Std: 0.0828
  Bands: Low (4.33%)  | Mid (93.16%) | High (2.51%)
  Features: Generated from coordinate distance-decay and alluvial formulas.
```

### Statistical Anomalies Identified in Jalandhar:
1. **Severe Mid-Band Compression:** $93.16\%$ of Jalandhar cells fall into the mid susceptibility band ($0.33 \le S < 0.66$). Standard deviation ($\sigma = 0.0828$) is less than half of Amritsar's ($\sigma = 0.1697$). Spatial contrast is largely eliminated.
2. **Flatness Skew:** Mean `f_flatness` in Jalandhar is $0.9073$ (compared to $0.5000$ in Amritsar) because `np.gradient` on a smooth linear slope yields near-zero gradient values, making $1.0 - \text{slope} \approx 0.90$ across the city.
3. **Low-Point Distortion:** Mean `f_lowPoint` is $0.6471$ in Jalandhar (versus $0.1206$ in Amritsar). Over $64\%$ of cells are treated as low points, defying hydrological reality.
4. **Uncalibrated Weight & Threshold Transfer:** Feature weights ($0.3, 0.1, 0.3, 0.15, 0.15$) and cutoffs ($0.33, 0.66$) calibrated on Amritsar's 13 hotspots were silently reused in Jalandhar.
5. **Verdict:** Susceptibility scores across cities **are not comparable**. $S = 0.50$ in Amritsar does not represent the same flood vulnerability as $S = 0.50$ in Jalandhar.

## 4. Machine-Readable Quality Observability Tooling (Phase 3)

We built an independent auditing CLI: `pipeline/scripts/17_city_quality_audit.py`, which evaluates any registered city and writes `public/data/cities/{city_id}/quality_report.json`.

### Audit Categorization Architecture:
- **Fatal Integrity Errors:** Malformed JSON, unnormalized weights, inverted bounding boxes, tampered `configHash`, invalid polygon geometries, duplicate cell IDs, scores outside $[0.0, 1.0]$. (Causes technical integrity gate to fail).
- **Warnings:** Rectangular AOI instead of official boundary, compressed score distributions ($\sigma < 0.10$), high flatness skew ($> 0.85$), anomalous low-point distribution ($> 0.50$), analytic coordinate proxies instead of physical rasters, cross-city score non-equivalence.
- **Evidence Limitations:** Zero historical flood incident inventory, lack of sub-daily rain gauge calibration, uncalibrated thresholds.

### Audit Summary:
- **Amritsar (`amritsar`):** Technical Integrity: **PASSED**, Calibration Status: **CALIBRATED**, Fatal Errors: **0**, Warnings: **0**, Evidence Limitations: **0**.
- **Jalandhar (`jalandhar`):** Technical Integrity: **PASSED**, Calibration Status: **UNCALIBRATED_EXPLORATORY**, Fatal Errors: **0**, Warnings: **6**, Evidence Limitations: **1**.

## 5. Defensible Jalandhar Evidence Plan (Phase 4)

We formulated a detailed, non-fabricating evidence collection protocol documented in `docs/JALANDHAR_EVIDENCE_PLAN.md`:

### Upstream Access Audit & Real Blockers:
1. **ERA5-Land Reanalysis (ECMWF via Open-Meteo):** Accessed & verified (14-year baseline at 31.33°N, 75.59°E). Shows rainfall presence, but cannot prove pluvial waterlogging occurred at a specific street.
2. **IMD AWS / ARG Telemetry:** Blocked. Sub-daily automated rain-gauge data requires Ministry of Earth Sciences institutional credentials.
3. **Municipal Corporation Jalandhar (MCJ) Complaint Logs:** Blocked. MCJ operates temporary paper/phone registers during monsoons; no open-data GIS or REST API exists.
4. **State SDMA / NDMA SACHET Feeds:** Partially accessible. Provides district-level alerts, but lacks street-level inundation coordinates.
5. **Local Newspaper Archives (*The Tribune*, *Hindustan Times*):** Accessible. Identified key historical choke points (Damoria Railway Underbridge, 120 Feet Road / Basti Guzan, Football Chowk, Burlton Park, Preet Nagar underpass).

### Decision:
Jalandhar remains strictly in `readinessState: "evidence_insufficient"`. No synthetic incidents were created. A 3-phase collection strategy (systematic media extraction, municipal partnership, and community crowdsourcing) is established to accumulate $\ge 5$ verified storm events before advancing to `evaluation_ready`.

## 6. Formal Calibration & Readiness State Machine Rules (Phase 5)

We formalized explicit entry conditions for all 6 readiness states:
1. **`configuration_only`:** Valid schema, bounding box, and verified `configHash`.
2. **`data_bootstrapped`:** Valid regular metric analysis grid in target UTM CRS.
3. **`model_generated`:** Scored susceptibility layer ($S \in [0, 1]$), `quality_report.json` passes technical integrity with 0 fatal errors.
4. **`evidence_insufficient`:** Model generated, but city possesses $< 5$ independent verified storm events in `incidents_inventory.json`. Alert delivery is strictly suppressed.
5. **`evaluation_ready`:** $\ge 5$ independent verified storms in inventory; localized historical backtesting completed with documented POD and sensitivity.
6. **`pilot_eligible`:** Evaluation ready, localized thresholds calibrated, municipal agency authorization recorded, operator sign-off granted. Default rollout mode remains `evaluation_only`.
